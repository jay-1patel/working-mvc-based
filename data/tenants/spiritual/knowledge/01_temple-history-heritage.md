# Sacred History & Heritage

The Divine Guide Lingam is believed to be swayambhu — it appeared on its own at this spot on the Ganga's bank. Local tradition holds that a wandering saint, meditating here, was guided in a dream to the exact place where the Lingam was found — which is how the temple received the name "Divine Guide".

The present stone temple was built about 250 years ago by a merchant family of Haridwar and has been maintained by the Divine Guide Temple Trust ever since.

The temple's brass bell (about 200 years old) and the carved wooden doors of the sanctum are protected heritage items — please do not touch the carvings.

The evening Ganga Aarti at the temple ghat steps has been performed without a break for over 100 years, including through floods and difficult years — a point of pride for the sevaks.

Every Mahashivratri, the temple holds a full-night jagran and four prahar abhisheks; in Shravan, jal is offered by thousands of Kanwar devotees.
