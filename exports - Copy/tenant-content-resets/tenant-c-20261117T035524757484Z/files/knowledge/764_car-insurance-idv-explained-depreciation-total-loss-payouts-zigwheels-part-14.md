The takeaway: IDV decides how much you get when the car is gone; zero depreciation decides how much you keep when it's fixed. A properly protected owner needs both pulling in the same direction.

Electric cars play by slightly different rules because a big chunk of their value sits in one component you can't see or easily fix: the battery pack. The battery is the most expensive part of an EV, and its value directly drives the premium. Motor repairs and other EV-specific hardware can also cost more than equivalent work on a petrol or diesel car.

That shakes up the IDV maths in three big ways:

Total loss can arrive sooner. If a flooded underpass or a nasty underbody hit damages the pack, the repair estimate alone can quickly reach the 75 percent of IDV threshold, especially on a car that's a few years old. An EV can be written off after damage that would only mean a long workshop stay for a petrol car.
