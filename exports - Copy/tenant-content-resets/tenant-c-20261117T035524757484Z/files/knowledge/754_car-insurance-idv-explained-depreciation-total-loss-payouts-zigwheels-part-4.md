Once the tow truck drives off with your car, the claim follows a fairly predictable script. You inform the insurer, a licensed surveyor arrives with a clipboard, and they assess the damage part by part. That inspection ends in one of two verdicts, and they could not be further apart.

It's repairable (partial loss): The car heads to the workshop, parts are fixed or swapped, and the insurer pays the bill minus parts depreciation, the compulsory deductible and anything excluded. You eventually get your car back.

It's not worth fixing (constructive total loss): The car is written off. No repairs, just a lump-sum payout, and you're back to car shopping.

A write-off also comes with homework. According to Section 55 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, the owner of a car damaged beyond use must inform the registering RTO within 14 days so the registration can be cancelled.
