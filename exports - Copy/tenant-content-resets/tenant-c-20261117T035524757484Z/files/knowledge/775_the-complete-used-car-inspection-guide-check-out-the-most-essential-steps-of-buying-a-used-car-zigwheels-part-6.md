Always remember to do a cold start, standing in front with the bonnet open. A cold start exposes problems a warm engine conveniently hides. A cold start can show you the car’s true colours, with common problems being tappet noise, injector chatter, turbo whine, or blue smoke on startup. If a seller insists the car is "already warmed up," that's not a favour; that's damage control or simply trying to lock the deal by hiding the real problem.

So you have now turned on the car and checked for unwanted sounds. Your next step shouldn’t be to drive right away. You should plug in an OBD scanner and check for stored fault codes, not just the ones currently active. It's also worth asking directly whether the fault code memory has been cleared recently, since that alone can hide a lot.
