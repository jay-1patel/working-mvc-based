Once that is done, it is time to finally drive the car. Once you start driving, try to pay attention to the minor but significant details. Observe if the car pulls to one side under braking, or if the shift from second to third gear happens cleanly, and whether the clutch bite point feels unusually high or low. For better observations, roll the windows down, turn the music off, and drive over a rough patch of road since suspension knocks are far easier to notice when there is less noise.

Even after you've decided to buy, the inspection isn't over. Check that both keys are present, along with the spare wheel, jack, toolkit, and the original owner's manual. Ensure to take photographs of the car from all four corners before you drive off, so there's a clear record of its condition at handover.
