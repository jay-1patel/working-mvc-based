Here's the twist most owners miss: your car's verdict, and how much money follows it, both trace straight back to that one IDV figure on your policy.

In a total loss, IDV stops being a line in the policy schedule and becomes the actual cheque. Under the policy, a car is a constructive total loss when repair costs exceed 75 per cent of the IDV, and the claim settles at that agreed IDV minus the deductible. Depreciation isn't applied again, but it has already done its damage by shrinking the IDV in the first place.

So IDV pulls double duty. It sets the tripwire for a write-off and caps the payout. A car insured for Rs 6 lakh is written off once repairs hit Rs 4.5 lakh, and the owner walks away with at most Rs 6 lakh, no matter what the same car costs on-road at the dealership that week.
